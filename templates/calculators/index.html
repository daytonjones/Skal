How These Calculations Work

Specific Gravity is determined by using a hydrometer.

Original gravity (OG) measures sugar before fermentation; final gravity (FG) measures residual sugar after. The difference lets you calculate alcohol.

In winemaking, the "standard" equation for calculating ABV (Alcohol By Volume) is typically used for most situations, while the "alternate" equation is used for higher-gravity wines where greater accuracy is desired. The standard equation is simpler and sufficient for most common wines, but the alternate equation provides a more accurate result for wines with higher alcohol content. The standard equation is a widely used, simpler formula. It works well for most wines, especially those with lower alcohol content.
The alternate equation is more complex but is designed to provide greater accuracy for higher-gravity wines. It accounts for the non-linear relationship between gravity and alcohol content in higher-alcohol beverages.
When to Use Which: Standard Equation: . Use for most standard wine fermentation, where the final ABV is not expected to be exceptionally high (e.g., below 12-14%). Alternate Equation: . Use when you are making a high-gravity wine, where the alcohol content is expected to be significantly higher, or when you need a more precise ABV calculation.

Basic formula:

ABV = (OG – FG) × 131.25

Alternate formula:

ABV = (76.08 × (OG – FG) / (1.775 – OG)) × (FG / 0.794)

Brix method: ABV = (OB – FB) × 0.55

Attenuation measures sugar converted to alcohol; lower means sweeter.

Calories in grams: (ml × ABV × 8)/1000, then ×7.
